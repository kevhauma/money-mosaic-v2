Back to income A bank CSV only ever records what landed in your account. Your gross wage — what you earned before tax and social contributions — never appears in it, which is why this is the one place on the Income page where you type rather than configure. Gross wage is that month's full pay before deductions. Filling it in unlocks the "Net vs gross" section: what share of your gross actually reached your account, and whether your raises are passing through or being eaten by a rising deduction rate. Months you leave empty simply leave a gap there — an unanswered month is not a zero. Bonus is the part of that month's deposit that was a 13th month, vacation or holiday pay rather than regular wage. Recording it does two things: it's subtracted before your take-home rate is worked out, so a bonus month can't read as a take-home rate you never had; and it's spread across its year on the income chart, so the spike you already explained stops being drawn as one. Use this when the bonus arrives inside your normal salary deposit — if it has its own category, tick that category under Income settings instead. Nothing here needs saving: leaving a cell writes it. Clearing both of a month's cells removes that month again — an amount of 0 is a real claim, and different from having said nothing.
